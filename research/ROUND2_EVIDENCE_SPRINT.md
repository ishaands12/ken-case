# 24-hour evidence sprint — Round 2

Do not invent results. This protocol is designed to produce first-hand evidence that directly changes Q2/Q3/Q6.

## Recruit

8–12 people who:
- chose a movement goal in the last 6 months; and
- missed or abandoned it at least once.

Prefer people outside the team. Record age band, goal type and interview date; anonymise quotes in submission.

## Part A — last real miss (5 minutes)

Ask only about a specific episode:
1. What exactly were you supposed to do, and when?
2. What happened in the two hours before it?
3. When did you first know you probably would not do it?
4. What did you do instead?
5. What happened the next planned session?
6. What would have made returning easier **in that moment**?

Do not ask “Would an AI help?” before this section.

## Part B — configure the prototype (5 minutes)

Give the participant the Goal Charter screen with no explanation beyond:
“Set this up as if it controlled your real goal.”

Capture:
- L2 or L3;
- evidence sources allowed;
- Recovery Credit / Stake / No Money;
- chosen caps;
- quiet hours;
- allowed actions.

The important evidence is what they configure, not what they say sounds interesting.

## Part C — scenario test (5 minutes)

Run in random order:
- calendar conflict;
- confirmed miss;
- ambiguous attendance.

After each, ask:
1. Was the agent allowed to do too much, too little, or the right amount?
2. What action would make you revoke it?
3. What evidence would you trust enough for money to move?
4. If the voice agent misheard you, what should happen?

For consequential L2 voice choices, test:
- speech-only confirmation;
- exact read-back + “yes”;
- keypad/DTMF confirmation.

## Part D — force the money trade-off

Ask with a concrete amount:

“You have ₹300 under your Goal Charter. After a verified miss, choose exactly one:
A. lose ₹50 as a Commitment Stake;
B. allow up to ₹180 to buy the smallest approved restart;
C. no money action.”

Then ask:
“Would you keep the same choice for four weeks?”

## Metrics to report if the sample supports them

- x/y chose L3 for free fallback.
- x/y chose L3 for paid re-entry.
- x/y chose L3 for a stake.
- x/y allowed calendar data.
- x/y allowed attendance proof.
- x/y selected Recovery Credit / Stake / No Money.
- x/y preferred DTMF or read-back when speech confidence was uncertain.
- x/y rejected the agent declaring failure from missing/ambiguous data.

## Evidence rule

Only put a claim in the final submission if:
- it came from the original 15-response survey; or
- you can trace it to these interviews/prototype sessions; or
- it is clearly labelled as a design hypothesis.

## Best possible new insight

We are specifically testing whether the deeper Round 2 insight is:

> People do not want one autonomy level. They grant more autonomy when the action is reversible and the proof is strong, and demand control as the action becomes harder to undo.

If the interviews do not support that, do not use it.
